Before the framework existed, "urgent" meant whoever asked loudest, or whoever asked last. Ten cross-functional teams, no shared definition of severity, and a Slack channel that treated a typo and a payments outage with the same panicked energy.

My first attempt at a fix was too complicated — five tiers, a decision tree, a form nobody filled out under pressure. It collapsed within a week because people reach for the fastest path when something's actually on fire, and mine wasn't it.

The version that stuck was boring on purpose: four tiers, one sentence each, no branching logic. P0 is revenue-down-right-now. P4 is "nice to fix eventually." Everything in between has a plain-language test, not a rubric. The goal was never precision — it was that anyone, at 2am, could self-classify in five seconds and be right often enough to trust.
