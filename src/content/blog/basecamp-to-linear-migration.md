On paper, this was a data-migration project: export from one tool, import into another, redirect the links. In practice, it was a change-management project that happened to involve software.

The resistance was never loud. Nobody argued in the kickoff. It showed up two weeks later as a shrinking number of updated tickets, as designers quietly keeping their real task list somewhere else, as "I'll move it over once things calm down." Calm never comes on its own schedule.

What actually got people to switch wasn't the migration itself — it was documenting the new workflow in the same breath as the tool, so the change was never just "we use a different app now" but "here's how your Tuesday looks different, and why it's better." Once the workflow made sense on its own terms, the tool stopped being the thing anyone had opinions about.
